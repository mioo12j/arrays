Solar for Assam's Tea Estates: Policy, Design and Lessons from Our Plants

Tea estates are among the best solar sites in India: daytime factory load, diesel back-up to cut and land to spare. Assam's policy, how to design for the plucking season, and what we have learnt building plants from Koomber to Harmutty.

Why tea estates suit solar so well

  • Daytime load. Withering, rolling, fermenting and drying run through the day in the plucking season.
  • Diesel back-up. Grid interruptions in rural Assam mean estates run DG sets; every solar unit that replaces diesel saves far more than a grid unit.
  • Land. Many estates have strips of non-plantation land near the factory, ideal for ground-mount plants.
  • Long horizon. Estates are run for generations, so a 25-year asset fits naturally.

Assam's policy in 2026

In September 2026 the Chief Minister, Dr Himanta Biswa Sarma, wrote that tea garden owners may use up to 5% of their land for solar generation, as part of the state's goal of 6,000 MW of installed green energy capacity by 2030. The state's solar promotion policy targets about 3,500 MW of solar by 2030, with a push for rooftop plants and agri-voltaics, and caps solar on tea-garden land at 5% of the landholding.

Assam's clean-energy policy for industry has also offered tea, coffee and rubber units buying power from APDCL features such as annual energy banking, so that surplus solar from the lean months can be drawn in the busy season, with grid-connected rooftop plants capped at 1,000 kW per location under that policy.

Check the current notification

Several Assam policies and orders apply, and their names and terms have changed over time. Confirm the current banking, capacity and approval rules with APDCL and the Assam Electricity Regulatory Commission before fixing a design.

Lessons from our tea-estate plants

  1. Plan around the season. Build in the lean months so the plant is ready before plucking peaks.
  2. Respect the estate. Work around labour lines, nurseries and drainage; keep the site clean and safe.
  3. Get the approvals moving early. APDCL feasibility, net metering and inspection take time.
  4. Engineer for rain. Assam's monsoon demands good drainage, raised equipment pads and proper earthing and lightning protection.
  5. Hand over properly. Train the estate electrician, leave drawings and test reports, and set a cleaning routine.

Frequently asked questions

Can tea gardens in Assam use their land for solar?
Yes. The Chief Minister has announced that tea garden owners may use up to 5% of their land for solar generation, and the state's solar policy caps solar on tea-garden land at 5% of the landholding.
Can solar work with a tea estate's diesel generators?
Yes. With a DG synchronisation controller the solar plant keeps running during grid outages alongside the generators, cutting diesel use.
Rooftop or ground-mount for a tea factory?
Ground-mount on spare non-plantation land is often better: it avoids old factory roofs and can be sized to the load. Rooftop works where the roof is sound and well oriented.
What size of plant suits a tea estate?
Most estates we work with install between about 200 kWp and 600 kWp, sized to the factory's daytime load in the plucking season and to grid limits.
