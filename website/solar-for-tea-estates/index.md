# Solar Power for Assam's Tea Gardens

19 on-grid solar plants and counting, for estates of Jay Shree Tea (BK Birla Group) and Goodricke Group.

Tea-Estate Solar

## Clean power for the tea industry

Tea estates draw heavy power in the processing season, much of it in daylight hours, and many rely on diesel generators when the grid fails. A ground-mount solar plant on estate land cuts both grid bills and diesel.

Arrays Ingenieria has become one of the most active solar builders in Assam's tea gardens. As reported in September 2026, the company has installed **19 on-grid solar plants** in the state's tea estates, from Jay Shree Tea's first-ever renewable project at Towkok and Manjushree to the Goodricke estates at Orangajuli and Barpatra.

### What tea estates need, and what we handle

- Ground-mount plants on estate land, on pile foundations
- Synchronisation with the estate's diesel generators
- Net-metering and approvals with APDCL
- Earthing and lightning protection for Assam's storms
- Module cleaning systems for easy washing
- Plants built on the CAPEX model, or as partner to programmes run by Tata Power Renewable Energy and Sustvest

### Estates we have powered

[Towkok (535 kWp) and Manjushree (500 kWp)](https://arraysingenieria.com/project-jayshree-tea-1mw-solar-assam/) for Jay Shree Tea; the Dewan, Labac and Burtoll gardens of Jay Shree Tea's Dewan Group; [Orangajuli (450 kW)](https://arraysingenieria.com/project-orangajuli-tea-estate-450kw-solar/) and [Barpatra (230 kW)](https://arraysingenieria.com/project-barpatra-tea-estate-230kw-solar/) for Goodricke; and Kakajan Tea Estate for Amalgamated Plantations. Most recently, the [595 kWp plant at Koomber Tea Estate](https://arraysingenieria.com/project-koomber-tea-estate-595kwp-solar-cm-inauguration/) was inaugurated by the Chief Minister of Assam on 1 October 2026.

Why It Matters

## Driving the PM's Panchamrit and Assam's green-energy goals

Tea processing is energy-intensive. Every estate that swaps grid and diesel power for solar cuts fossil-fuel use, contributing to Prime Minister Narendra Modi's "Panchamrit" climate commitments announced at COP26: 500 GW of non-fossil capacity and half of India's energy needs from renewables by 2030, and net zero by 2070.

It also advances Assam Chief Minister Dr Himanta Biswa Sarma's target of 6,000 MW of installed green-energy capacity by 2030, and his decision to let tea gardens use up to 5% of their land for solar. Sustainable innovation and clean energy can drive the next chapter of Assam's economic growth story.

Sources: [PM's national statement at COP26, PIB, 1 Nov 2021](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1768712); [Himanta Biswa Sarma, 22 Sep 2026](https://himantabiswa.substack.com/p/rewriting-assams-energy-future).
