Solar O&M and Module Cleaning: Protecting 25 Years of Returns

A solar plant has no moving parts, but it still needs care. Module cleaning, inspections, thermography, inverter and earthing checks, and a practical maintenance schedule that keeps generation where it should be.

Key takeaways
  • Dust and bird droppings can cut output noticeably between cleanings, especially in dry, dusty months
  • A fixed module cleaning system (pipes, valves and nozzles) makes regular washing quick and safe
  • Track the performance ratio monthly; a falling ratio is the first sign of a problem
  • Thermography and string-current checks find hot spots, failed bypass diodes and loose connectors early
  • Earthing resistance and lightning protection should be tested every year, before the monsoon

Why O&M decides your return

A solar plant's return comes from the units it generates over 25 years. Losing even a few per cent of output every year to dirt, tripping inverters or failed strings adds up to years of lost payback. Good operation and maintenance (O&M) protects that output, and keeps the plant safe.

Module cleaning: how, how often and with what

Cleaning options compared
MethodHow it worksSuits
Manual with hose and brushCrews wash with water and soft brushesSmall plants; sites with easy, safe access
Fixed module cleaning system (MCS)A permanent HDPE or uPVC pipe network with valves and hose points across the arrayRooftop and ground plants of any size; fast and safe
Robotic cleaningWaterless or low-water robots run along the rowsLarge, uniform utility plants

Cleaning frequency depends on dust, rain and bird activity: often every one to two weeks in dry, dusty months and less in the monsoon. Use soft or demineralised water where possible, never abrasive tools, and clean in the early morning or late afternoon so cold water does not hit hot glass.

What we install

Most of our plants include a module cleaning system: an HDPE or uPVC pipe network from the water tapping point to hose points across the array, so the estate or factory team can wash the modules safely without walking on them.

The maintenance schedule

Reading the numbers: performance ratio

The performance ratio (PR) compares what the plant actually generated with what it should have generated for the sunlight it received. It strips out the weather, so a falling PR points to a plant problem: dirt, a failed string, an inverter derating, or shading from new growth. Track it monthly and act when it drops.

Common faults and what causes them

  • Hot spots on modules: cracked cells, failed bypass diodes or persistent shading; found by thermography.
  • Low string current: a loose MC4 connector, a damaged cable or a dirty or shaded row.
  • Inverter trips: grid voltage excursions, insulation faults (often moisture in junction boxes) or overheating.
  • Earth faults after rain: damaged DC cable insulation or water ingress; test insulation resistance.

Frequently asked questions

How often should solar panels be cleaned in India?
Typically every one to two weeks in dry, dusty months and less often in the monsoon. Watch generation: a drop after dry spells shows when cleaning is due.
What is a module cleaning system?
A permanent network of pipes, valves and hose points across a solar array that lets crews wash modules quickly and safely without walking on them.
What is a good performance ratio?
Well-built and well-maintained plants commonly run at about 75% to 85%, depending on climate and design. The trend matters more than the figure.
Do solar plants need maintenance during the monsoon?
Less cleaning, but more electrical attention: check earthing, lightning protection and insulation before and after heavy rain.
